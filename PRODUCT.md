# Pixel & Paint

## Register
brand

## Users
Community-minded parents, educators, local nonprofits, and potential sponsors in Fresno's Central Valley who are looking for free creative enrichment programs for youth. They value authenticity, grassroots energy, and tangible community impact over polished corporate messaging. They discover the organization through Instagram, word of mouth, and local community boards.

## Product Purpose
Pixel & Paint brings free art and technology pop-ups to kids in low-income housing communities in Fresno, California. The website exists to communicate the mission clearly, showcase past events, attract volunteers and sponsors, and give families a way to find upcoming pop-ups. Success is a parent sharing the link in a neighborhood group chat saying "my kid loved this."

## Brand Personality
Three-word personality: **joyful, resourceful, community-rooted**. The voice is plainspoken, hopeful, and active. It sounds like a neighbor who happens to be an artist, not a nonprofit grant application. Headlines are short and direct. Claims are real and local. The brand trusts that showing the work speaks louder than explaining it.

## Anti-references
Explicitly avoid these patterns:
- **Generic nonprofit template.** Stock photos of smiling diverse children, muted earth tones, serif headlines, "Empowering communities through..." language. This is the default mode for every community org site and communicates nothing specific.
- **Overly corporate philanthropy aesthetic.** Gradient hero sections, "Impact Report" language, donor-centric framing that centers the giver over the community.
- **Minimalist tech startup.** Clean white space, geometric sans-serif everything, abstract illustrations. This brand is colorful, tactile, and handmade—not sterile.
- **AI-default warm cream SaaS.** Fraunces headlines, peach gradients, soft rounded everything. The brand is bold, saturated, and has hard edges.
- **Cluttered community bulletin board.** While the energy is grassroots, the execution should be intentional and designed. Avoid visual chaos, mismatched fonts, or unstructured layouts.

## Design Principles
1. **Color is the message.** The saturated palette (yellow, pink, blue, green, orange) communicates joy and energy before a single word is read. Color is structural, not decorative.
2. **Handmade over templated.** Pixel clusters, hard-edge shadows, slight rotations, and navy keylines give the design a crafted, physical quality—like a screen-printed poster, not a Canva export.
3. **Community over institution.** The site should feel like it belongs to the neighborhood, not to a boardroom. Photography, language, and layout should center the kids and the art, not the organization.
4. **Accessible by default.** WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Large touch targets, readable type, motion respect. The audience includes families with older devices and varying connectivity.
5. **Show, don't explain.** Event photos, kid artwork, and real moments carry more weight than mission statements. Lead with visuals, support with words.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.1 AA minimum across all text, headings, and interactive controls. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for all animation. Color is never the sole carrier of meaning. Tap targets ≥ 44px on mobile. Reading order matches visual order. The site must perform well on budget Android devices over cellular connections.
